# Offer and pricing rules

The user's plans and prices live in `project.json → offer` and appear in every proposal.
These rules keep them credible. Example figures below are **examples only**; use the
user's own.

## Three plans

| Plan | For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Entry ("Launch") | Small beauty, food and one-person trades | The page they've already seen, finished with them; tap-to-call, map, reviews; live in 14 days |
| Recommended ("Growth") | Default for trades, dental, legal, real estate | Everything in entry plus Google Business Profile tune-up, local service pages, structured data |
| Custom (quote) | Several locations, online booking or ordering | Scoped on the demo call |

For high-ticket niches (legal, dental, medical, real estate, HVAC) **present the
recommended plan first**. An entry-price site can look cheap to a lawyer or dentist. Fall
back to the entry plan if needed.

## Never discount on the phone

A discount on the call contradicts the price in your own proposal and teaches the owner
that waiting gets a better deal. **Lower the scope, never the price.** If the budget is
tight, offer the entry plan, or a split payment.

## Founding-client deal (first 3 clients only)

Zero clients means zero proof. Offer the recommended plan at the entry price **in exchange
for** a two-line testimonial you may quote and permission to show their page as a case study
in your portfolio (before/after, with their name). It's a trade with a reason, not a
discount, and it buys the proof that makes clients 4–20 easier. Say exactly how many
founding slots exist, and end it when they're gone.

**Never trade the price, a discount or a gift for a Google review** (or any public review).
Google's review policy forbids incentivised reviews, and consumer-protection rules in many
countries treat them as fake reviews. After delivery you may *ask* every client for an
honest review, with nothing attached: same price, same service, whether they write one or
not, and whatever they write.

## Care plan (recurring)

A monthly plan for hosting, small edits and Google profile updates (example: $25–60 a
month depending on market). One-time sales don't add up to a business; 20 clients on a care
plan is steady monthly income. Every site has to be hosted somewhere, so every client needs
this answer anyway. Offer it at the close, not on the first call.

## Deposit

- A deposit (typically 50%) books the build slot. The balance is due when they approve
  the finished page.
- Make paying easy on the same call: bank transfer details, a payment link, or a local
  instant-payment method. Prepare it before the first call (`templates/playbook/close-kit/`).
- Optional guarantee: if they don't like the finished page, refund the deposit. At small
  amounts this costs little and removes the main reason to wait.
- Only "deposit received" counts as a client.

## Real reasons to act now (only if true)

- **Honest scarcity:** "I build {n} sites a week, and I have {n} slots starting {date}."
- **A real deadline:** a founding price that ends on a date, a price rise you will actually
  make, or a build slot. Never invent one.
- **Reward speed, not haggling:** a small bonus for a deposit within 48 hours of the demo
  (for example, a free Google Business Profile clean-up). It costs you an hour, not money.
- **Seasons:** name the one that matters to their business.

| Niche | Season trigger (adjust for hemisphere and climate) |
|---|---|
| HVAC | Heating season (autumn), cooling season (late spring) |
| Plumbing | First deep freeze (frozen pipes) |
| Pest control | Autumn (pests move indoors), spring |
| Roofing, landscaping | Spring; after storms |
| Restaurants, caterers | Holiday parties (bookings happen 6–10 weeks before), patio season |
| Salons, beauty | Weddings and graduations (spring), holidays |
| Accountants, tax | Weeks before tax deadlines |
| Real estate | Spring market (prepare in late winter) |
| Auto | Winter tyre changeover, pre-holiday road trips |
| Dental, medical | Start of the year (new benefits), back to school |

## Cost-of-waiting maths (on the call)

```
jobs to break even = price ÷ value of one job
monthly cost of waiting ≈ extra jobs per month the site would bring × value of one job
```

Use the owner's numbers. Example (fictional): job value $400, entry price $900 → 2.25 jobs
to break even; if the new page brings one extra call a month that becomes a job, it pays
for itself in about two months.

## What the price must include (say it)

Timeline, what they provide (logo, photos, hours, domain login), number of revision
rounds, who owns the site and domain, what happens if they stop the care plan.
