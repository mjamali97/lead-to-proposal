# Everything else that stands between you and paying clients

Ordered roughly by how much each moves the first deposits.

## Before the first call

**Make "yes" finishable on the same call** (about 2 hours, once). Prepare a one-page
agreement, payment instructions, an invoice template and a short onboarding form (logo,
photos, hours, domain login). Without them, a yes cools into "send me the details".
Templates: `templates/playbook/close-kit/`.

**Fix what owners see when they look you up** (about 3 hours). After a cold call, many
owners search for you. Your own site needs a page for this exact service, 3–5 example
pages (as images), your packages and prices, a phone number and a real name and city. A
studio site that sells something else, or shows nothing, sends a mixed message right when
they're deciding to trust you.

**Use a diagnosis tool as the opener** (5 minutes per lead). The evidence line from
`l2p queue` is the start. For trades and professional services, also check whether an AI
assistant or a search for "best {service} in {city}" names them. Only use what you checked
that day.

**Send PDFs during the call, never links.** The one-page landing-page PDF during the call;
the full proposal PDF after the demo. On the demo, share your screen and click through the
live page yourself. They see it working, but get nothing they can hand to someone else to copy.

**Call in order of visible need, not list order.** No website, social-only, free
subdomain and broken sites first; then niches in season; then the rest.

## This month

**Founding deal for the first three clients.** Recommended plan at the entry price in
exchange for a review, a testimonial and permission to show the page
(`offer-and-pricing.md`).

**A monthly care plan.** Hosting, small edits, Google profile updates. Recurring income.

**A referral engine** (1 hour a week). A reward (cash or a free month of care) for every
referral who signs. Ask every client and every polite "no" for names. Recruit 3–5
partners who already serve small business owners: accountants, bookkeepers, insurance and
mortgage brokers, print shops, business associations. Friends and freelancers are
referral sources, not buyers: ask them for names.

**Walk in** (one afternoon block a week). Restaurants, cafés and salons rarely answer the
phone at the right time. Visit 8–10 places between lunch and dinner with the page open on
your phone. Ask for the owner; leave nothing printed that can be copied.

**One case-study post a week** where your prospects already gather (local business
groups, community forums, social media). A before/after of a real client page (with
permission) beats a general ad. Follow each group's rules; no unsolicited DMs to members.

**Split selling from building.** The usual failure: calls stop the week the first client
signs, because building takes over. Keep call blocks fixed. Build in other blocks, at
weekends, or hand builds to a partner.

**A 30-minute review every Friday.** Scoreboard, weakest step, one change for next week
(`templates/playbook/weekly-review.md`).

## Stop doing

| Stop | Why |
|---|---|
| Discounting on the phone | It contradicts your own proposal and rewards waiting |
| Sending a proposal without a booked time | "Send it" is how busy owners end calls |
| Selling to friends and freelancers | They refer; they rarely buy first |
| Chasing "I'll build it myself" and "we have a team" | One check-in date, then move on |
| Open-ended callbacks | "Evening" isn't a time. Ask "7:15 or 8:00?" |
| Bulk messages and cold email blasts | Messaging apps ban numbers that mass-message strangers; anti-spam law applies to every commercial email |
| Pitching defects you didn't see | One wrong claim costs the whole conversation |

## Anti-spam and platform cautions

- **One-to-one only.** Every message is written for one business and sent by a person.
  This plugin never sends anything, on purpose.
- **Messaging apps** (WhatsApp, Telegram, Messenger) restrict or ban accounts that message
  many strangers or get reported. Message after a call where the owner agreed, or a number
  the business publishes for messages. Use a business profile. Keep volume human.
- **Email law:** commercial email needs your identity, postal address and a working
  opt-out in most countries; some countries require consent first, even for businesses
  (see `docs/COMPLIANCE.md`). Honour "no unsolicited email" statements.
- **Calls:** check your country's do-not-call rules and calling hours; keep your own
  do-not-call list (`l2p queue --stop`).
- **Suppression is permanent.** When anyone says stop, on any channel, run
  `l2p queue --stop` the same day.
